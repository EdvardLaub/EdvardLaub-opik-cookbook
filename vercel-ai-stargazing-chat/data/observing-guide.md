# Lantern Ridge Observing Guide

A fictional field guide for the members of the Lantern Ridge Astronomy Circle. Everything here is invented for demonstration.

## Dark adaptation
Allow 25 minutes for full dark adaptation at the Ridge field. Use only red light below 620 nm, and set a torch to its lowest level. A single glance at a phone screen resets about ten minutes of adaptation. Lantern Ridge members keep a red-film "Ember cap" over any screen.

## Choosing a site
The Ridge field has a Bortle 3 sky, about 21.5 magnitudes per square arcsecond at the zenith. The car park at Millstone Gate is Bortle 5 and fine for the Moon and planets. The north horizon at the Ridge is blocked by the Ash Barn, so targets below 15 degrees of altitude in the north are not visible there.

## Seeing and transparency
The Circle rates seeing on the Hollis scale from 1 (boiling) to 5 (steady). Hollis 4 or better is needed for planetary detail at more than 200x. Transparency is judged by the faintest star visible in the bowl of the Little Dipper: stars to magnitude 5 or fainter mean good transparency.

## Loaner telescopes
The Circle lends two instruments. "Pip" is a 4.5-inch tabletop reflector, f/4, with a 25 mm eyepiece (about 18x) and a 10 mm eyepiece (about 45x). "Marlow" is an 8-inch Dobsonian, f/6, with a 30 mm (about 40x) and a 12 mm (about 100x) eyepiece. Collimate Marlow before each session; the collimation cap is in the lid pocket. Loans are one week, collected from the Ridge hut.

## Beginner targets by season
- Spring: Leo Triplet galaxies (use Marlow and averted vision), Mars when above 20 degrees, the Beehive cluster.
- Summer: Lyra's Ring Nebula (needs Marlow's 100x), Albireo double star, the Milky Way core from the Ridge field.
- Autumn: the Andromeda Galaxy (visible to the eye from the Ridge), Double Cluster in Perseus, Saturn when well placed.
- Winter: Orion Nebula (fine in Pip), Pleiades, Jupiter and its four brightest moons.

## Planning a session
Check the cloud forecast, the Moon phase and the Hollis seeing forecast posted on the Circle board each Friday. Arrive 45 minutes before the planned start to set up and dark-adapt. A session is called off when forecast cloud cover exceeds 60 percent or wind exceeds 35 km/h, because Marlow's mount vibrates above that.

## Safety and etiquette
Never point any instrument at the Sun without a certified solar filter; the Circle does not lend one. Keep voices low and headlights off after dusk. Park in the marked row at the Ridge field so car lights do not sweep the observing area. The hut kettle is for members only.

## Photography basics
Pip and Marlow are visual instruments. Phone photography through the eyepiece works for the Moon with a 25 mm eyepiece and a steady hand. Deep-sky long exposures are not possible on a Dobsonian mount without a tracking base, and the Circle owns none.
