# Bottling and Carbonation

## Priming sugar
For 20 litres of finished beer, dissolve 120 g of table sugar (sucrose) in 250 ml of boiling water, cool it, and stir it gently into the bottling bucket. This carbonates to about 2.4 volumes of CO2, right for a pale ale. For a wheat beer use 150 g.

## Bottling steps
Rack the beer onto the priming solution without splashing, because oxygen at this stage makes the beer taste of wet cardboard within weeks. Fill each bottle to the top of the bottling wand, which leaves a headspace of about 3 cm when removed. Cap immediately.

## Conditioning
Store bottles upright at 20 to 22 C for two weeks to carbonate, then move them somewhere cool for another week. Chill one bottle before you open it. A beer that is still flat after three weeks usually had too little yeast left or was conditioned too cold.

## Bottle bombs
Never bottle before fermentation has finished. A gravity that is still falling means the yeast will keep producing CO2 and the bottles can burst. Use only bottles meant for beer, never decorative or screw-top bottles from mineral water.
