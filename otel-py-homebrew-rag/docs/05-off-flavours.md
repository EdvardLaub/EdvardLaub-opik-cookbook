# Off-Flavour Field Guide

## Buttery or butterscotch (diacetyl)
Slick, buttery flavour comes from diacetyl. Causes: fermentation that finished too cold or was cut short, or bottling before the yeast could clean up after itself. Fix: leave the beer on the yeast for three extra days at 20 C.

## Green apple (acetaldehyde)
Fresh-cut green apple means the beer was moved off the yeast too soon. Give young beer more time before racking and bottling.

## Medicinal or plastic (chlorophenols)
A band-aid or antiseptic taste usually comes from chlorinated tap water. Add one crushed Campden tablet per 20 litres of brewing water, and let it sit for ten minutes before heating.

## Hot, solvent-like (fusel alcohols)
A warming, nail-polish-remover note means fermentation ran hot, above 23 C. Control temperature in the first 72 hours, when the yeast is most active, for example with a tub of water and a wet towel.

## Sour and tart
Unplanned sourness is infection, not style. See the sanitising chapter.
