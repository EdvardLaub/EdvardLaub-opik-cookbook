# VHF Marine Radio

## Distress calls
In a life-threatening emergency, switch to channel 16, press the distress button if you have DSC, and say "Mayday, Mayday, Mayday" followed by your vessel name, position, the nature of the emergency, and the number of people aboard. Repeat until someone answers.

## Urgent and safety calls
Use "Pan-Pan" for urgent situations that are not life-threatening, such as a disabled engine in calm weather. Use "Securite" for navigation or weather warnings. Both are announced on channel 16 and then moved to a working channel.

## Channel 16 and working channels
Channel 16 is the calling and distress channel and must be monitored whenever the radio is on. Make first contact on 16, then agree on a working channel such as 68 or 72, and switch. Keep calls under one minute and never use channel 16 for chatting.

## Radio checks and licensing
Request radio checks on channel 68 rather than 16. A vessel station licence is required for ships that travel on international voyages; recreational boats on local waters need only a restricted operator certificate for the person using the radio.

## Range
VHF is line of sight. Expect roughly 5 nautical miles between two small boats, and 20 nautical miles to a coast station with a tall antenna.
