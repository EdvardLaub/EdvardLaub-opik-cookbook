# Weather and Sea Conditions

## Checking the forecast
Check the marine forecast before departure and again at midday. Pay attention to wind speed, gusts, wave height and the timing of fronts. The Harbor Authority publishes a forecast on VHF weather channel WX2 and at the harbormaster's office, updated every 6 hours.

## Small craft advisory
A small craft advisory is issued when sustained winds of 18 to 33 knots or waves above 1.2 metres are expected. Boats under 8 metres should stay in port during an advisory. Larger vessels should go out only with an experienced skipper and a clear plan to turn back.

## Thunderstorms
If lightning is visible, head for shore immediately. If caught on the water, lower antennas if possible, keep everyone low in the boat's centre, and wear life jackets. Do not touch metal fittings or the VHF radio during close strikes. Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before resuming.

## Fog
In fog, reduce speed to the point where you can stop within half the visible distance. Sound one prolonged blast every 2 minutes when underway, and post a lookout at the bow. Use radar or a plotter to avoid shipping lanes.

## Cold water
Water below 15 degrees Celsius can cause cold shock within the first minute and incapacitate swimmers within about 10 minutes. If you fall in, stay with the boat, keep your jacket on, and adopt a fetal position to preserve heat.
