# Right of Way and Navigation

## Head-on situations
When two power-driven vessels approach head-on, both must alter course to starboard (right) and pass port side to port side. Make the turn early and large enough that the other skipper can see it. Sound one short blast to signal a turn to starboard.

## Crossing situations
When two power-driven vessels cross, the vessel that has the other on its starboard side is the give-way vessel and must keep clear. The vessel on the right is the stand-on vessel and holds its course and speed unless a collision becomes imminent.

## Overtaking
A vessel overtaking another must keep clear of the vessel being overtaken, whichever side it passes on. The overtaken vessel holds course and speed. Signal your intent with two short blasts to pass on the port side, one short blast to pass on the starboard side.

## Sail versus power
A sailing vessel under sail alone generally has right of way over a power-driven vessel. The exceptions are when the sailboat is overtaking, or when the power vessel is restricted to a narrow channel or is towing or fishing. Never assume the other skipper has seen you; keep clear if in doubt.

## Speed in harbors and no-wake zones
Inside marked harbor limits the speed limit is 5 knots, and you must produce no wake within 60 metres of docks, moored boats, or swimmers. You are legally responsible for any damage your wake causes.
